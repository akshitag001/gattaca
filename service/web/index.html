The pipeline

Six stages of real computer vision.

Scroll to watch an actual run. Every frame below is genuine OpenCV 5 output from this project's own pipeline — rendered on a synthetic hand, so no real person's biometrics appear in this demo.

01 / INPUT

01 Input

A single ordinary photo. No special hardware, no scanner — exactly what a camera or a social post would capture.

02 Detect

A neural hand-landmark model runs through cv2.dnn to find fingertips, with a classical skin-mask and convexityDefects fallback.

03 Rectify

The distal phalanx is cropped, perspective-corrected and unrolled from its cylinder, then rescaled so one ridge spans ~9 px — the 500 ppi forensic standard.

04 Enhance

CLAHE, denoising, a structure-tensor orientation field and a bank of oriented getGaborKernel filters. This is where ridges either appear — or prove they were never there.

05 Extract

Binarise, thin to a one-pixel skeleton with ximgproc.thinning, then find ridge endings and bifurcations by crossing number.

06 Score

A calibrated model turns clarity, resolution and feature count into HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW — plus a heat map showing where you're exposed. Never a usable print.

Measured, not guessed

The internet says “1.5 metres.”
We actually measured it.

Popular articles repeat unsourced claims with no methodology. We ran a controlled study — 60 fingers × 47 degradation conditions, matched against paired ground truth — and parameterised it by pixels per ridge period, the only variable that physically matters.

3.9 px

Pixels per ridge needed for 95% identification. Below ~2.9 px, recovery collapses.

~1 px

Lost to a single JPEG q75 re-encode — platform resizing is accidental protection.

0.87 px

What a 12 MP phone actually resolves at 1.5 m — below Nyquist. The folklore is wrong.

LOW→MED

Fusing 6 video frames promotes a finger a whole risk band. A clip leaks more than a photo.

Claim vs. reality

Same pose. One is fiction.

Viral posts "prove" the risk with zoomed-in fingerprints. In one widely shared report, the zoom is literally captioned "AI-generated image." We made one the same way, then ran the real photo through GATTACA.

Honest caveat: a closer, sharper shot of a fingertip can expose real ridge detail. That's what the measured curve above quantifies, and why the tool below exists.

    For the curious

    What a fingerprint actually is, digitally.

    Forensic systems rarely store a picture of a ridge. They store a compact minutiae template: the points where ridges end or split, each as a position, an angle and a type.

    ISO/IEC 19794-2 · FINGER MINUTIAE RECORD 1 finger · showing 3 of 34
    #x (px)y (px)angletypequality
    011829641° ridge ending 74
    02204158126° bifurcation 88
    0397233309° ridge ending 61
    A full record also carries a header (format id, capture device, image size, ppi) and optional ridge-count and core/delta data. Related: ANSI/NIST-ITL 1-2011 (exchange) · WSQ (500-ppi compression).
    GATTACA deliberately does not emit this. It reports how many ridge features it found and how clearly — never their coordinates, never a template, never a clean enhanced print you could feed to a matcher. A privacy-awareness tool must not become the extraction tool it warns about.
